Student musicians and the university

Starting observation

A public connection

  • Student musicians perform in contexts where they are publicly connected to their university.
  • Some may say they represent it. Others may say they simply perform music. Others may use completely different language.
To support with evidenceVerify the exact NU events and the major concert outside the university before presenting them.

Research question

How do student musicians experience performing at university events and outside campus, and how do they understand their connection to the university through these performances?

Main question: performance experience. Analytical focus: the university connection.

Research question

The case: NU Orchestra

  • A large student organization.
  • Performs at university events and outside the university: different contexts within one case.
  • I do not assume students see themselves as representing NU, or that a connection feels positive or negative.

Epistemological positioning

Interpretivism

  • I want students' own meanings and interpretations.
  • Two students in the same performance can understand it differently: pride, "just a musician", responsibility, pressure.
  • Those differences are the finding, not variables to control.

A positivist design would fit a different question: does performing outside campus increase belonging or responsibility?

Contribution

A student-centered account

  • How student musicians understand performing in university and outside-campus contexts, including whether and how they connect it to the university.
  • Extends research on music participation and student belonging.
  • Small and situated, with thick description so others can judge whether it resembles their context (transferability).

Analytical framework

Representation as a sensitising concept

  • It is the phenomenon I care about, but I do not assume participants use or accept it.
  • It tells me what to notice, not what I will find.
  • Other aspects may come up in accounts: belonging, responsibility, connection between students, preparation, repertoire.

Analytical framework

My expectations, and I could be wrong

  • Performing outside campus may make students more aware of a connection to the university.
  • Some may feel pride or responsibility.
  • But I may be assuming the association matters to them at all.

Written down before the first interview, to compare with what participants say.

Analytical framework: positionality

Near the orchestra, not inside it

  • An NU student, not a member of NU Orchestra.
  • Helps: shared context, easier conversation.
  • Risks: taking university experience for granted; participants leaving things unexplained because they assume I understand.
  • I first met the orchestra through its concert content and online community: a public-facing image, not necessarily what members experience.

Analytical framework: positionality

What I will do about it

  • Ask students to describe a performance first, and raise the university only afterwards if they have not.
  • Ask them to explain what seems obvious to me.
  • Write a reflexive note after each interview and compare it with my expectations.
