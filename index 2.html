𝄞 Student musicians and the university

How student musicians understand their performances, and their connection to the university. An interpretivist study.

Theme · starting observation

A public connection

  • Student musicians perform in contexts where they are publicly connected to their university.
  • Some may say they represent it. Others may say they simply perform music. Others may use completely different language.

Movement I · research question

How do student musicians experience performing at university events and outside campus, and how do they understand their connection to the university through these performances?

Main question: performance experience. Analytical focus: the university connection. I do not assume students see themselves as representing NU, or that a connection feels good or bad.

Movement II · epistemological positioning

Interpretivism: same performance, different music

“Proud to represent NU.”
“Simply performing as a musician.”
“Responsibility, even pressure.”
  • I take an interpretivist approach because I want to understand how student musicians themselves interpret their experiences.
  • Different students can live the same performance in different ways. I treat that variation as meaningful, not something to resolve.

I want to understand students' own meanings rather than impose my own definition of what representation means.

Movement II · epistemological positioning

What this means for how I study it

  • What I am not doing: measuring whether performing increases belonging or responsibility, or looking for one objective effect. That would be a positivist question.
  • What counts as knowledge: the meanings students give their experiences, in their own words. Only they can tell me how they see it.
  • My own role: I am part of how meaning is made in an interview, so I do not claim to be neutral. I use reflexivity: I write down my expectations and check them against what students say.
  • Good evidence: rich detail (thick description) and interpretations I can show from students' words, not counts.
  • Limits: I show how these students understand it, and readers judge whether it applies to their own setting (transferability).
  • Thick description: describing an experience with enough detail and context that a reader understands what it meant to the person.
  • Reflexivity: noticing and writing down how my own background and expectations shape what I ask and hear.
  • Transferability: giving enough detail for readers to judge whether the findings might fit their own setting.

Movement III · contribution

A student-centered account

  • How student musicians understand performing in university and outside-campus contexts, including whether and how they connect it to the university.
  • Extends research on music participation and student belonging.
  • Small and situated. Thick description lets others judge whether it resembles their context (transferability).

Movement IV · literature review

Three conversations

Belonging

Dost and Mazzoli Smith (2023) brought together qualitative studies (a meta-ethnography) on how students themselves understand belonging to their university.

Why it matters: belonging is treated as the students' own meaning.

Music making

Kokotsaki and Hallam (2007) asked 78 music students what music making did in their lives. It was a social act that brought belonging and a feeling of contributing to a group.

Why it matters: it links music making with belonging.

Representation

Matthews and Dollinger (2023) argue that student representation and student partnership are different, using two points of difference: responsibility and access.

Why it matters: it shows how representation is usually understood.

Movement IV · the debate

What does representation mean?

Matthews and Dollinger argue that representation and partnership differ, and that the difference matters. Press Next to reveal each idea.

♫ Partnering with staff

Students working together with teachers and staff on teaching and learning. The authors say this is not the same as representation.

♩ Publicly connected

Students connected to the university through performing. This is what I study, without assuming they see it as representing.

"I just play music." For me this is data too, not a problem.

Movement IV · the gap

What is still unheard

  • Belonging is studied through students' own views.
  • Music making is linked to belonging and to contributing to a group.
  • Representation is discussed mostly as students speaking for other students.
  • In the sources I have so far, these conversations sit apart. My study brings them together by asking how student musicians themselves understand their performances and whether they connect them to their university.

Provisional until my full literature search is finished.

Movement V · analytical framework

Representation as a sensitising concept

It tells me what to notice, not what I will find. These aspects, including organising events and preparing for them, may come up in students' accounts; their own words may lead off the map.

A sensitising concept gives a general direction for what to notice. A measured concept is defined in advance and turned into a variable to look for.

Movement V · expectations

What I expect, and could be wrong about

  • Performing outside campus may make students more aware of a connection to the university.
  • Some may feel pride or responsibility.
  • But I may be assuming the association matters to them at all.

Written before the first interview, to compare with what participants say.

Movement V · positionality

Where I stand

  • An NU student studying a student community within my own university.
  • Helps: shared context, easier conversation.
  • Risks: taking university experience for granted; participants skipping explanations because they assume I understand.
  • I first came to this topic through public concert content and online communities: a public-facing image, not necessarily what students experience.
  • So: ask for a description of the performance first, ask them to explain the obvious, and write reflexive notes after each interview.

Encore · not graded

What working with AI helped me understand, and what I still don't understand about my research design

Clearer now

Wording carries assumptions: "representing their university" and "role" both built in something students should be free to reject. A student who says "I just play music" gives me data, not a problem. Representation can guide what I notice without being the answer I expect.

Still in the fog

How can I keep representation as an important focus without leading students toward seeing themselves as representatives, if they do not see their performance that way? And what exactly do my sources say? I am still reading them.
